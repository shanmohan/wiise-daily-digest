Google's A2A Protocol Reaches 150 Production Organisations, Now Governed by Linux Foundation

Source: The Next Web — Google Cloud Next 2026 Coverage

Google's Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol, which enables cross-platform agent communication across different frameworks and vendors, has reached production deployments at 150 organisations and has been elevated to version 1.2 with cryptographic signatures for domain verification. The protocol is now governed by the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, lending it a neutral, open-standards imprimatur that has attracted Microsoft, AWS, Salesforce, SAP, and ServiceNow as production users. Crucially, A2A is designed to complement rather than displace Anthropic's Model Context Protocol (MCP): MCP handles how an agent connects to tools and data sources, while A2A handles how fully separate agents communicate across organisational boundaries — a division of labour that positions both standards to coexist in enterprise stacks.

Google Unveils TurboQuant at ICLR 2026: A KV-Cache Compression Breakthrough for Long-Context Models

Source: Crescendo AI News, May 2026

Google's research team presented TurboQuant at ICLR 2026, an algorithm that substantially reduces the memory overhead of the key-value (KV) cache — one of the primary bottlenecks preventing large context-window models from being deployed economically at scale. The method combines a PolarQuant vector rotation step with Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss compression, allowing models to handle massive context windows while consuming significantly less memory per layer. Researchers argue the breakthrough could accelerate a broader industry shift away from raw parameter-count scaling toward efficiency-first development, with direct implications for on-device AI deployment and data centre operating costs — both areas where the economics of current frontier models remain challenging for widespread enterprise rollout.
