1. Agentic AI & Frameworks

LangGraph Consolidates Its Lead as the Production-Grade Agent Standard

Alice Labs / PEC Collective / AgileSoftLabs — May–June 2026

Across multiple independent production assessments in 2026, LangGraph has emerged as the consensus top pick for complex, stateful agent workflows requiring audit trails, conditional branching, and human-in-the-loop checkpoints. Its graph-based architecture — where agents, tools, and checkpoints are explicit nodes — gives teams deterministic control that role-based frameworks cannot match at scale. The framework reached v1.0 in late 2025 and has since surpassed CrewAI in GitHub stars, driven strongly by enterprise adoption in regulated industries such as finance and healthcare.

MCP and A2A Protocols Graduate to Linux Foundation Governance

Daily.dev / The New Stack — March–June 2026

The Model Context Protocol (MCP), which now has over 5,800 public servers and 70% of major SaaS brands offering remote MCP endpoints, has been joined under Linux Foundation governance by Google's Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol — together forming a two-layer standard the community describes as "MCP gives your agent hands; A2A gives your agents colleagues." The AGENTS.md convention, which turns a repository into an agent's onboarding guide, has also been adopted by Codex, Cursor, Copilot, and Windsurf as a cross-tool portability standard, though Claude Code still reads its own CLAUDE.md file. This convergence is beginning to shift competitive differentiation away from raw model quality and toward orchestration, governance, and tooling ecosystem depth.

CrewAI Commercialises Enterprise Tier While AutoGen Rebrands Under Microsoft

AgileSoftLabs / Fungies.io — March–April 2026

CrewAI continues to be the fastest path from concept to working multi-agent prototype, with its open-source MIT core remaining free while enterprise features — including a management UI, role-based access control, and managed deployments — move to a paid tier. Microsoft's AutoGen has meanwhile undergone a significant structural shift: the community-maintained v0.2 lineage now lives as AG2, while Microsoft's own v0.4 rewrite introduces an event-driven, async-first execution model well-suited to Azure-integrated conversational agent systems. Industry data suggests that by mid-2026, roughly 68% of enterprise development teams have moved beyond simple AI assistants to deploy full agentic systems in production, up from 51% the previous year.

Google ADK's A2A Support Positions It as the Interoperability Layer for Cross-Framework Agents

GuruSup / AlphaMatch — April 2026

Google's Agent Development Kit (ADK), released in April 2025 and now maturing rapidly, has carved out a distinctive niche through its native support for the A2A protocol — allowing ADK-built agents to discover and invoke agents constructed with LangGraph, CrewAI, or any other A2A-compliant framework via standardised task interfaces. The hierarchical agent tree model, where a root agent delegates to sub-agents that can themselves spawn further sub-agents, integrates tightly with Vertex AI and Google Cloud services. For teams deeply invested in the Google ecosystem, ADK now offers a compelling path to heterogeneous multi-framework agent orchestration, though its orchestration features remain lighter than LangGraph for production-hardened workflows.

Kili Technology / BenchLM.ai — May–June 2026

The research community's response is the proliferation of harder evaluations: Humanity's Last Exam, with 2,500 questions designed by domain experts, holds the best AI models to around 35% accuracy while human domain experts average 90%, exposing a 50-plus-point gap that older benchmarks entirely miss. Enterprise practitioners face an additional concern: independent analysis shows a 37% gap between lab benchmark scores and real-world production performance, with a 50× cost variation across models achieving similar stated accuracy on standardised tests.

EU AI Act Enforcement Delayed 16 Months — Relief for Enterprise Rollouts, but Only Temporary

Gloat / Baker Donelson — May 2026

In a significant regulatory development, EU lawmakers reached a provisional agreement on May 7, 2026 to overhaul key provisions of the AI Act as part of the broader Digital Omnibus package — pushing back enforcement of high-risk AI system obligations from August 2, 2026 to December 2, 2027, a 16-month extension. For AI systems classified as regulated products or safety components, the deadline extends further to August 2028, and the package includes broader regulatory relief for smaller businesses and clarified rules around processing sensitive data for bias detection purposes. The reprieve gives enterprise teams more runway for compliance preparation, but organisations that treat this as a signal to de-prioritise governance work entirely risk being caught flat-footed when the stricter obligations eventually take effect.
