LangGraph / LangChain

Source: AliceLabs.ai & AgileSoftLabs Blog — April–May 2026

After years of rapid experimentation, the agentic framework landscape has matured around clear production leaders. The AI agent ecosystem has matured significantly in 2026, with three frameworks emerging as clear leaders: LangChain's LangGraph for complex orchestration, CrewAI for team-based workflows, and Microsoft's Agent Framework (successor to AutoGen) for enterprise conversational agents. Google ADK & A2A Protocol

Google's A2A Protocol Reaches 150 Production Organisations — Linux Foundation Takes Governance

Source: The Next Web — Cloud Next 2026 coverage, May 2026

Google's Agent-to-Agent interoperability standard has crossed a major maturity milestone. Google's A2A protocol, originally launched with more than 50 technology partners, has reached 150 organisations in production — not pilot — routing real tasks between agents built on different platforms. The protocol is now governed by the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation and has reached version 1.2, with signed agent cards using cryptographic signatures for domain verification. Microsoft, AWS, Salesforce, SAP, and ServiceNow are running A2A in production environments. A2A is designed to complement rather than compete with Anthropic's Model Context Protocol (MCP): MCP handles how an agent connects to tools and data sources, while A2A handles how agents communicate with each other across organisational and platform boundaries. The standout feature of Google ADK is native support for the A2A protocol, which enables communication between agents from different frameworks — an ADK agent can discover and invoke an agent built with LangGraph or CrewAI through A2A's standardised task interface.

CrewAI / AutoGen

CrewAI and AutoGen/AG2 Battle for the Accessible Middle Ground as Framework Consolidation Accelerates

Source: ATNO for GenAI & Agentic AI, Medium — April 2026

While LangGraph dominates the enterprise tier, CrewAI and AutoGen are competing for teams that prioritise speed and accessibility. LangGraph surpassed CrewAI in GitHub stars during early 2026, driven by enterprise adoption and its graph-based architecture that maps cleanly to production requirements like audit trails and rollback points. State-Level Regulation

Colorado AI Act Takes Effect June 30 — Cyber Insurance Market Now Demands Documented AI Security Controls

Source: Wilson Sonsini / Kiteworks — January–May 2026

Enterprises face a tightening compliance clock as the first substantive US state AI laws approach enforcement dates. The Colorado AI Act, currently slated to come into effect on June 30, 2026, will place substantial new responsibilities on AI developers and deployers, including requirements to undertake reasonable care to avoid algorithmic discrimination, develop a risk management policy and program, implement notices, and conduct impact assessments. Simultaneously, the risk insurance market is using market power to drive AI governance adoption: insurance carriers are increasingly conditioning coverage on the adoption of AI-specific security controls, with many now requiring documented evidence of adversarial red-teaming, model-level risk assessments, and alignment with recognised AI risk management frameworks before they will underwrite policies — leaving organisations without robust AI security practices potentially uninsurable or facing prohibitive premiums. Enterprises should note that despite the Trump Administration's efforts to pre-empt state AI laws, these regulations remain enforceable until formally struck down, meaning businesses should comply with applicable state laws rather than waiting for federal courts to resolve jurisdictional disputes.
